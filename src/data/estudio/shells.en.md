## What they are

A **shell voicing** has only three notes: the **root** in the bass and the two **guide tones**, the **3** and the **7**. It is the minimum for the chord to sound complete: the root says where we are and the 3 and 7 say which chord it is (see [Guide tones](../notas-guia/)). The 5 is left out because it adds no colour.

It has been the basic comping voicing of jazz guitar since the big-band era: it sounds clear, leaves room for the bass and the soloist, and moves from one harmony to the next with very little effort.

## The four shapes

With the root on the 6th or 5th string, and the 3 or the 7 just above the bass:

| Root on | Order | Strings | Cmaj7 | C7 | Cm7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6th | 1 · 7 · 3 | 6-4-3 | `8 x 9 9 x x` | `8 x 8 9 x x` | `8 x 8 8 x x` |
| 6th | 1 · 3 · 7 | 6-5-4 | `8 7 9 x x x` | `8 7 8 x x x` | `8 6 8 x x x` |
| 5th | 1 · 3 · 7 | 5-4-3 | `x 3 2 4 x x` | `x 3 2 3 x x` | `x 3 1 3 x x` |
| 5th | 1 · 7 · 3 | 5-3-2 | `x 3 x 4 5 x` | `x 3 x 3 5 x` | `x 3 x 3 4 x` |

(Frets from the 6th to the 1st string; x = not played.)

- The **1-7-3** shapes sound more open (the 7 low and the 3 on top, like a horn section); the **1-3-7** ones, more compact.
- In **sixth** chords (6, m6) the 6 takes the place of the 7; in **sus**, the 4 takes the place of the 3; in the **diminished** (°7), the bb7.
- To practise one specific shape in every quality, also see the **Chords** section.

## How they connect

The key is **alternating root on the 6th and root on the 5th**. In a ii–V–I (Dm7 G7 Cmaj7, the example above in C):

- Dm7 with the root on the 5th: x 5 3 5 x x (1-b3-b7),
- G7 with the root on the 6th, in the same area: 3 x 3 4 x x (1-b7-3),
- Cmaj7 again with the root on the 5th: x 3 2 4 x x (1-3-7).

The hand hardly moves: the guide tones make their usual journey (the 7 drops a half step to the 3, the 3 stays as the 7) and the bass jumps in fourths between the 5th and 6th strings. The example above chooses the shapes of any progression this way: of all the possible ones, the sequence that moves the hand and the voices least.

## How to practise them

1. **The four shapes in one quality.** Choose the key and play maj7, 7, m7 and m7b5 in the four shapes. Say the degrees out loud as you change.
2. **ii–V–I in all twelve keys.** Start with Dm7 on the 5th string; then start with Dm7 on the 6th (the rest change shape). Go round the cycle of fifths.
3. **Rhythm.** One hit per beat with the left hand muting between hits (Freddie Green style: short, on all four beats, no accents). Then the Charleston (1 and the "&" of 2) and anticipations on the "&" of 4.
4. **With bass.** If you play with a bass player, drop the root and play only the 3 and 7: it is the same shell without the bass, with even more space.
5. **Add a tension.** Turn on *With a tension* in the example: a 9 or a 13 goes on top of the shell (b9 on the altered dominant, 11 on m7b5). Four notes that already sound like a modern jazz chord.
6. **The top voice.** Pay attention to the highest note of each shell: it is a melody. Change shape (1-3-7 or 1-7-3) so that melody moves up or down by step.
7. **In the repertoire.** Play the standards in the Repertoire with shells only before using fuller chords. If a chord does not come quickly as a shell, that is the one you need to practise.

## Ideas for variety

- **Shell + melody note.** Play the shell on the low strings and the tune's melody on top, on the high strings: the beginning of chord melody.
- **Walking bass line.** Play the root on beat 1 and passing notes (chromatic or from the scale) on beats 2, 3 and 4 towards the root of the next chord; the shell only on beat 1.
- **Shells on the high strings.** The 3 and 7 on strings 3-2 or 2-1, without the root: to comp behind another guitarist without stepping on their register.
- **The next step.** When shells come naturally, move on to four-note voicings in [Drop 2](../drop2/).
