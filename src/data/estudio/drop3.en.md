## What it is

Like [drop 2](../drop2/), but dropping the **third voice from the top** of close position by an octave (in C E G B, the E):

| | From low to high |
|---|---|
| Close | C · **E** · G · B |
| Drop 3 | **E** · C · G · B |

In other words, the bass is **separated** from the rest: there is almost an octave between it and the next note, and the three upper voices stay together. On the guitar the bass goes on the 6th or 5th string and the other three on adjacent strings, **skipping one string**:

- group **6-4-3-2** (the 5th string is not played),
- group **5-3-2-1** (the 4th string is not played).

It sounds like a bass plus a small chord on top: very useful for playing **without a bass player** or solo, and for comping with the root in the bass. It is the voicing of the "classic" jazz chords with the root on the 6th string, like Cmaj7 at 8 x 9 9 8 x.

## Inversions and string groups

Each chord has four inversions depending on the bass note. Cmaj7 on 6-4-3-2 (frets from the 6th to the 1st string; x = not played):

| Inversion | Bass | Frets | Notes | Top |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2nd inversion | 5 | `3 x 2 4 1 x` | G E B C | 1 |
| 3rd inversion | 7 | `7 x 5 5 5 x` | B G C E | 3 |
| Root position | 1 | `8 x 9 9 8 x` | C B E G | 5 |
| 1st inversion | 3 | `12 x 10 12 12 x` | E C G B | 7 |

Cmaj7 on 5-3-2-1:

| Inversion | Bass | Frets | Notes | Top |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Root position | 1 | `x 3 x 4 5 3` | C B E G | 5 |
| 1st inversion | 3 | `x 7 x 5 8 7` | E C G B | 7 |
| 2nd inversion | 5 | `x 10 x 9 12 8` | G E B C | 1 |
| 3rd inversion | 7 | `x 14 x 12 13 12` | B G C E | 3 |

- The most used are the **root positions** (root in the bass) on both groups: with them you can play almost any standard.
- As with drop 2, the shapes work for every quality by changing one or two notes: in C7 the B drops to Bb; in Cm7, the E also drops to Eb.
- The inversions with the 3 or the 7 in the bass give bass lines that move up or down by step (like the *Stepwise descending bass* in Progressions).

## How they connect

**With the root in the bass**, alternating 5th and 6th strings (the example's *Root in the bass* option, with *Both* groups). ii–V–I in C:

| | Dm7 | G7 | Cmaj7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frets | `x 5 x 5 6 5` | `3 x 3 4 3 x` | `x 3 x 4 5 3` |
| Notes | D C F A | G F B D | C B E G |

The bass goes D → G → C and, inside, the guide tones do the usual: C (7 of Dm7) drops to B (3 of G7) and F (3 of Dm7) stays as the 7 of G7; then B stays as the 7 of Cmaj7 and F drops to E. It is the same movement as [shell voicings](../shells/) with one more note on top.

**With the smallest movement**, without the option: the inversions that move the upper voices least are chosen, and the bass tends to move by step instead of jumping in fourths.

## How to practise them

1. **Root positions on 6-4-3-2 and 5-3-2-1.** maj7, 7, m7, m7b5 and °7 with the root on the 6th; then with the root on the 5th. Say which note is on top in each one (in the 6-4-3-2 root position, the 5).
2. **ii–V–I alternating strings.** Start with the ii on the 5th string, as in the table; then start with the ii on the 6th. Go through all twelve keys.
3. **The inversions of one quality.** The four maj7 ones on each group, climbing the neck and saying which note is in the bass.
4. **Bass lines.** With *Root in the bass* turned off, listen to the bass line in a long progression (Rhythm changes, Turnaround III–VI–II–V): you will see it tends to move by step.
5. **Play without a bass player.** A whole standard from the Repertoire in drop 3 with the root in the bass: the bass on beat 1 and the rest of the chord on 2 and 4 (stride or bossa nova style, depending on the tune).
6. **Combine with drop 2.** Drop 3 on chords that fall on a strong beat or last a whole bar, drop 2 on passing chords. That way the hand stays close and the density of the sound changes.

## Ideas for variety

- **Tensions.** Replace the top 5 with the 13 on dominants (in G7 3 x 3 4 5 x: the D becomes E) or the root with the 9 on m7 chords.
- **Melody on top.** Find the inversion with the tune's melody note in the top voice: drop 3 leaves the bass free and the melody on top, the basis of chord melody.
- **Bass + guide tones.** Play only the bass and the two guide tones of each shape (drop the top voice): it is a shell voicing, and it shows you where drop 3 comes from.
