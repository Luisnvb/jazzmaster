## Chord tones, tensions and avoid notes

All 12 notes fit over a chord, but they don't all play the same role:

- **Chord tones** (1, 3, 5, 7): a safe place to rest.
- **Tensions** (9, 11, 13 and their alterations): the scale notes that aren't in the chord. They add colour without changing the function; you can **rest** on them in the melody and put them in the **voicing**.
- **Avoid notes**: scale notes that sit **a half step above** a chord tone. The b9 interval between the two clashes, and the upper note pulls towards the lower one: it sounds like a note that has to resolve. They aren't forbidden: **in passing** they sound fine; what you avoid is resting on them or putting them in the chord.
- **Outside the scale**: chromatic notes. They work in passing or as an **approach** to a chord tone (see [Chromatic approaches](../aproximaciones/)).

The **half step above** rule sums up almost everything: the 11 over a chord with a major 3 (Cmaj7, C7) clashes with the 3; the b9 over a Phrygian m7 clashes with the root; the Aeolian b13, with the 5. A half step **below**, on the other hand, doesn't clash: it is an approach (the major 7 under the root, the #9 under the 3).

## Chord by chord

| Chord | Scale | Tensions | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imaj7 | Ionian | 9 · 13 | 11 |
| IVmaj7 | Lydian | 9 · #11 · 13 | — |
| IIm7 | Dorian | 9 · 11 · 13 | (13, as a long note) |
| IIIm7 | Phrygian | 11 | b9 · b13 |
| VIm7 | Aeolian | 9 · 11 | b13 |
| ImMaj7 | Melodic minor | 9 · 11 · 13 | — |
| V7 | Mixolydian | 9 · 13 (and altered) | 11 |
| bVII7, IV7, subV7 | Lydian dominant | 9 · #11 · 13 | — |
| V7alt | Altered | b9 · #9 · #11 · b13 | (9, 13 and 5 are outside) |
| V7sus4 | Mixolydian | 9 · 13 (b9) | 3 |
| IIø7 | Locrian #2 | 9 · 11 · b13 | (b9, in Locrian) |
| °7 | Diminished (W–H) | 9 · 11 · b13 · 7 | — |

Three things to notice:

- **The major chord doesn't take the 11.** That is why maj7 with an 11 is written **maj7#11**: Lydian removes the clash and leaves the brightest tension.
- **The minor chord takes almost everything.** With the b3, the 11 is a step and a half away and doesn't clash: m11 is one of the most open chords. Dorian's 13 is the only doubt on the IIm7 of a ii–V (with the b3 it forms the tritone of the coming V7).
- **The dominant is the most flexible.** Its 11 clashes with the 3 (except in sus4, where the 4 replaces the 3), but it takes the **four alterations**: b9, #9, #11 and b13. Which to use depends on where it resolves: towards major, the 9 and 13 sound good; towards minor, the b9 and b13 (which are notes of the minor tonic).

## Why the b9 clashes

A half step is the most tense interval, but **it depends on the octave**: a major 7 (B over C) sounds good in Cmaj7, and its inversion, the b9 (C over B, more than an octave apart), sounds harsh. That is why it matters which note is **on top**: the upper tension should be at least a whole step above the chord tone below it. A natural 11 over the 3 forms a b9 (F over E); a #11 forms a 9 (F# over E), which doesn't clash.

The dominant is the exception: the b9 over the root is **exactly the tension** you're after, because the chord wants to resolve.

## How to practise it

1. **Listen to all 12.** Choose a chord above and play *All 12 over the chord*: hear how the chord tone rests, the tension colours, the avoid note wants to move and the outside note sounds like a mistake if it stays.
2. **Sing the tension.** Play the chord and sing each tension above it. Then sing it without hearing it first.
3. **Resolve the avoid notes.** Over Cmaj7, play F and take it to E; over G7, play C and take it to B. It is the 4-to-3 movement of all tonal music.
4. **Voicings with tensions.** In [shell voicings](../shells/), add a tension on the top string: 9 or 13 on maj7, 9 or 11 on m7, 9, 13 or an altered one on 7. Never the natural 11 on a maj7 or a 7.
5. **Melodies on tensions.** Improvise over a fixed chord resting only on tensions (chord tones only in passing). It is the "modern" sound of jazz.
6. **Dominants by destination.** In a major ii–V–I, try the V7 with 9 and 13; in a minor ii–V–i, with b9 and b13. Listen to how the tension anticipates the tonic.

## Ideas for variety

- **One tension per chord.** In a progression from the repertoire, choose a tension for each chord and make a line with them, with as little movement as possible.
- **Avoid notes as appoggiaturas.** Write phrases that land on an avoid note on the strong beat and resolve a half step down on the weak one.
- **Compare scales for one chord.** C7 with Mixolydian, Lydian dominant and altered: the same root, three colours (in [Scales](../escalas/)).
