## The idea

Playing **outside** means deliberately leaving the harmony for a moment and **coming back**. It isn't playing wrong notes: it is creating strong tension that resolves when you return to the key, like a chromatic approach but on the scale of a whole phrase.

It works through **contrast**. The accompaniment stays inside the whole time; the soloist leaves with a clear idea and comes back. The ear hears both keys at once, notices the clash and feels relief on the return. If the excursion is too long, or the idea can't be recognised, it stops sounding intentional and sounds like a mistake.

The simplest way, and the basis of all the others, is **half-step displacement** (*side-slipping*): playing a phrase that works over the chord, repeating it a half step higher or lower, and returning to the original. It is the sound of 1960s modal jazz (Coltrane, McCoy Tyner, Herbie Hancock) and of saxophonists like Michael Brecker.

In the example above you hear the same four-bar phrase in two ways: **all inside** and **going out** in bar 2. The background chord does not change. Notes outside the scale are marked.

## Techniques for going out

- **Half step up.** The whole phrase a half step higher: over Dm7, think Ebm7. Almost every note is outside, and on the way back everything resolves down a half step. The most used.
- **Half step down.** The same but below (C#m7 over Dm7). It sounds like a long chromatic approach rising into the key.
- **Tritone.** The phrase a tritone away (G#m7 over Dm7): the most distant key, very tense. Over a dominant it is the tritone substitution and sounds altered (Db7 over G7).
- **Chromatic staircase.** A piece of the idea climbing by half steps (a half step, a whole step…) and falling back into the key. Very effective because the ear follows the sequence even as it moves away.
- **Others:** superimposing the pentatonic or triad of another key, symmetric patterns (diminished, whole tone, fourths) or following a motif while transposing it freely.

## How to make it sound intentional

1. **Go out with a clear idea.** A short, recognisable motif, ideally one you have already played inside: the listener hears it move and understands it is on purpose.
2. **Stay out briefly.** One bar, two at most. The return is what matters.
3. **Come back resolving.** The first note back should be a chord tone, a half step from the last note outside. The half step is what makes the arrival felt.
4. **Firm rhythm.** Outside, the rhythm has to be surer than ever. If the rhythm hesitates, the excursion sounds like you got lost.
5. **In the right place.** It works better over long chords (vamps, modal tunes, the I of a blues) than over fast changes, and better in the middle or at the end of a solo than at the beginning.

## How to practise it

1. **Hear the difference.** In the example, play "Both in a row" with each technique and each cell. Pay attention to the moment of return (bar 3).
2. **Play it yourself.** With the loop and the background chord, play the original phrase and then the displaced one, in a single position on the neck: a half step up is moving your hand one fret.
3. **Question inside, answer outside.** Two bars inside and the same idea a half step up. Then the other way round: question outside, answer inside.
4. **In a modal tune.** Over *Cantaloupe Island* (the Fm7 bars) or the Cm section of *Footprints*, go a half step up in the last bar of each chord and resolve as you enter the next.
5. **Record yourself.** If, listening back, it isn't clear that you go out on purpose, the idea wasn't clear enough or the excursion was too long.

## Ideas for variety

- **Combine with motifs:** transpose a motif a half step, a whole step and a tritone (see [Motifs and development](../motivos/)): each transposition is a different way out.
- **Outside pentatonics:** over Dm7, alternate the D minor pentatonic with the Eb minor one (see [Pentatonics in jazz](../pentatonicas/)).
- **Going out on the dominant:** in a ii–V–I, the V7 is the natural place to go out: the phrase a tritone away (tritone substitution, Db7 over G7) or the major pentatonic on its b5 (Db over G7) sound altered and resolve to the I by half step.
- **Approaches on a large scale:** think of the excursion as a one-bar [enclosure](../aproximaciones/): above, below and inside.
