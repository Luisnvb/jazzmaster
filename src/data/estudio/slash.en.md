## The idea

A **slash chord** is written with a slash: **D/C** is a D triad with C in the bass. There are two very different cases:

- **Inversion:** the bass is a note of the triad (C/E, C/G). It is the same chord with another note on the bottom.
- **A new chord:** the bass is not in the triad (D/C, Bb/C). The triad's notes become **tensions** over the bass, and the result is another chord: D/C sounds like C Lydian (9, #11 and 13 over C).

An **upper structure** is the same thing, but over a complete chord instead of a bass note alone: with the root, 3 and 7 of C7 underneath (the *shell*) and a D triad on top, you get C13(#11). The idea is powerful because a triad is easy to see and play, and well chosen it gives two or three tensions at once.

In the example above you combine the triad (its root, as an interval over the bass, and its type) with a bass or a base chord, and you see the resulting chord symbol, the degrees the triad adds and how it sounds.

## Most used slash chords

In C:

| Slash | Chord it sounds like | Triad degrees | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| D/C | C6/9(#11), no 3rd | 9 #11 13 | open ending, Lydian |
| Bb/C | C9sus4 | b7 9 11 | the modern sus |
| Gm/C | C9, no 3rd | 5 b7 9 | soft dominant |
| Db/C | Csus4(b9,b13) | b9 11 b13 | Phrygian, Spanish sound |
| E/C | Cmaj7#5 | 3 #5 7 | Lydian augmented |
| Eb/C | Cm7 | b3 5 b7 | the minor chord written as a triad |

The two most useful are **9sus4** (the major triad a whole step below the bass: Bb/C, F/G, D/E) and **Lydian** (the major triad a whole step above: D/C, G/F).

## Upper structures over a dominant

Over C7 (C, E and Bb underneath), each major triad gives a dominant with different tensions:

| Triad | Result | Tensions it adds | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| D (II) | C13(#11) | 9 #11 13 | Lydian dominant |
| A (VI) | C13(b9) | 13 b9 3 | diminished (half-whole) |
| Gb (bV) | C7(b9,#11) | #11 b7 b9 | diminished |
| Ab (bVI) | C7(#9,b13) | b13 1 #9 | altered |
| Eb (bIII) | C7(#9) | #9 5 b7 | #9 with the 5: blues |
| Dbm (bII minor) | C7(b9,b13) | b9 3 b13 | F harmonic minor |

To remember them: **II and VI** give natural or nearly natural tensions (dominants that don't resolve or that go to major); **bV, bVI and bIII** give altered tensions (dominants resolving to minor or with tension). It works the same over maj7 and m7: V or II major over Cmaj7 (maj9, maj13(#11)), IV or bVII major over Cm7 (m13, m11).

## On the guitar

On the guitar you can't play a full chord and a close-position triad at the same time. Three practical ways:

- **Open bass or bass on the 6th/5th string and the triad on the high strings.** For slash chords over a bass: C on the 5th string and the D triad on strings 3-2-1.
- **Someone else plays the bass.** With a bass player or pianist, play just the triad: the bass gives the root and you the tensions. It is the most common way to use upper structures in a band.
- **Triad plus the 3 or the 7.** Play the triad on the high strings and add just the 3 or the 7 of the base chord on the 4th string: enough for the dominant to be heard.

## How to practise it

1. **Hear the base and then the triad.** In the example, play "Base only" and then "With the triad": you will hear what the triad adds.
2. **The six on a dominant.** Go through the upper structures over C7 in the table (press each row) and learn the name of each one by its sound.
3. **Triads on the high strings.** In [Triads](../triadas/), find each structure's triad on strings 3-2-1 and play it with the chord's root on the 5th or 6th string.
4. **In a ii–V–I.** Over the G7 of a ii–V–I in C, play the Eb triad (the bVI of G: G7(#9,b13), altered) and resolve to the C or G triad over Cmaj7.
5. **Read the slash chords in the repertoire.** Many modern tunes write slash chords: think of each one as "triad + bass".

## Ideas for variety

- **Move the triad over a fixed bass:** over a C pedal, play D, Eb, E, F… and listen to how the colour changes (see [Quartal voicings](../cuartas/), which does the same with fourths).
- **Chain structures with voice leading:** from D/C7 to the nearest triad over Fmaj7, as in [Drop 2](../drop2/).
- **Improvise with the triad:** the upper triad's arpeggio is a line with the chord's tensions (see [Triad pairs](../pares-triadas/)).
