## The 12-bar form

The blues is **12 bars in three four-bar phrases**, and each phrase has a function (see [Functional harmony](../armonia/)):

| Bars | Basic | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | I7 · IV7 · I7 · I7 | **Tonic**: the I settles in. |
| 5–8 | IV7 · IV7 · I7 · I7 | **Subdominant**: it goes to the IV and comes back. |
| 9–12 | V7 · IV7 · I7 · V7 | **Dominant**: V, IV on the way back and the V that leads to the top. |

Two oddities make it unmistakable:

- **Everything is dominant.** I7, IV7 and V7 all carry the b7. In a major key that makes no sense (the I7 "wants" to go to the IV), but in the blues it is the colour: the b7 and the b3 of the melody are the **blue notes**. That is why the analyser marks the I7 as a *blues tonic* and, when it goes to the IV, as V7/IV.
- **The IV in bar 2** (*quick change*) is optional: the basic one above uses it; without it, the first four bars are I7.

## Four variants

All four share the same skeleton: each one **adds chords that prepare** those of the previous one, without changing where the harmony goes. Compare them bar by bar in the table above (the marked cells are the ones that change from the basic blues).

- **Basic.** I7, IV7, V7. The blues of Chicago, rock and roll and the early boogies.
- **Jazz.** The basic one with ii–Vs. In bar 4, **Vm7–I7** is the ii–V of the IV. In bar 6, **#IV°7**: a passing diminished that rises to the I of bar 7. In bar 8, **VI7** (V7/II) leads to the **IIm7–V7** of bars 9 and 10, the ii–V–I of the key instead of V7–IV7. And at the end, the **turnaround** I7–VI7–IIm7–V7. It is the one in *Tenor Madness*, *Now's the Time*, *Billie's Bounce*…
- **Bird blues.** Charlie Parker (*Blues for Alice*, *Laird Baird*): it starts on **Imaj7**, not I7, and descends through **secondary ii–Vs**: VIIø–III7 towards VIm, VIm–II7, Vm7–I7 towards IV. In bar 6, **IVm7–bVII7** (the backdoor); in bars 7 and 8, **chromatic ii–Vs** dropping a half step (IIIm7–VI7, bIIIm7–bVI7) down to the IIm7 of bar 9. The skeleton stays: bar 5 on the IV, 9–10 on the ii–V, 11–12 turnaround.
- **Minor.** Im7 and IVm7 instead of I7 and IV7. In bar 9, **bVI7**: tritone substitute of V7/V, falling a half step to **V7alt**. It is the one in *Mr. P.C.*, *Equinox*, *Footprints* (in 6/4)…

## What to play over it

- **Basic blues**: the **minor pentatonic** or the **blues scale** on the root works over all 12 bars; the b3 over the I7 is the "blue note". To sound more jazzy, switch to each chord's **Mixolydian** and aim for its 3 (the 3 of I7 and the b7 of IV7 are a half step apart: the blues [guide tone](../notas-guia/)).
- **Jazz blues**: mix. The blues scale for colour, but mark the changes: the 3 of each dominant, the ii–V in bar 4 and the one in bars 9–10.
- **Bird blues**: one scale for everything won't do. Play **arpeggios and guide tones** for each ii–V; the passing dominants accept the [altered tensions](../tensiones/) (b9, #9, b13).
- **Minor blues**: **Dorian** or minor pentatonic over Im7 and IVm7; on the bVI7, **Lydian dominant**; on the V7alt, **altered**.

## How to practise it

1. **Learn the form by ear.** Loop the basic blues and count the bars without looking: you have to feel where the IV arrives (bar 5) and the V (bar 9).
2. **Compare one variant with another.** Play the basic blues and then the jazz one, and listen for which new chords appear. Do the same with the jazz one and the Bird blues. At each change, ask yourself which chord it prepares.
3. **Analyse.** Write the function of each chord of the variant you are playing; the *Analyse* button opens it in [Functional harmony](../armonia/).
4. **Comping.** Comp with [shell voicings](../shells/) and then in [drop 2](../drop2/), with the Charleston rhythm (in [Rhythm](../ritmo/)).
5. **Improvise in layers.** First the blues scale only; then guide tones; then arpeggios; then mix. One chorus each.
6. **In every key.** In jazz, F and Bb above all; in rock and blues, E, A and G. Then all twelve.

## Ideas for variety

- **Reharmonise the basic blues yourself**: add a ii–V before each chord (in [Reharmonization](../rearmonizacion/) with the *secondary ii–Vs* technique) and compare it with the jazz one.
- **Mix variants**: bars 1–8 of the jazz blues with bars 9–12 of the Bird blues.
- **8- or 16-bar blues**: find blues tunes in the repertoire with another form and locate where the IV and the V are.
