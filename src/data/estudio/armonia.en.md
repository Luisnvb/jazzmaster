## Three functions

In a key, each chord fulfils one of three **functions**:

- **Tonic (T)**: rest. The place you return to.
- **Subdominant (SD)**: departure. It leaves the rest point without yet creating tension.
- **Dominant (D)**: tension. It wants to resolve to the tonic.

Almost all tonal music is a **T → SD → D → T** cycle: the perfect cadence (I–IV–V–I), the jazz ii–V–I, the turnaround… Knowing each chord's function is knowing **where the harmony is going**, and that matters more than the chord's name.

| Function | In major | In minor |
|---|---|---|
| Tonic | Imaj7 · IIIm7 · VIm7 | Im7 · bIIImaj7 |
| Subdominant | IIm7 · IVmaj7 | IIø7 · IVm7 · bVImaj7 · bVII7 |
| Dominant | V7 · VIIø7 | V7 · VII°7 |

Chords with the same function are **interchangeable** because they share notes: IIIm7 (E G B D) has three notes of Cmaj7 (C E G B); IIm7 (D F A C) has three of Fmaj7 (F A C E); VIIø7 (B D F A) is the four notes of G9 without the root.

## Why the dominant pulls

What makes V7 want to resolve is the **tritone** between its 3 and its 7: in G7, B and F. Both notes move a half step towards the tonic: B rises to C and F falls to E (the [guide tones](../notas-guia/)). Any chord that has that tritone works as a dominant:

- **VII°7 or VIIø7**: V7 without its root.
- **Tritone substitute (subV7)**: Db7 has the same F and B (Cb), with the roles swapped. That is why IIm7 – bII7 – Imaj7 sounds so natural.

## Chords from outside the key

Jazz harmony uses many chords that are not in the scale, but almost all of them have a clear function:

- **Secondary dominants (V7/x)**: the V7 of another chord in the key. VI7 leads to IIm (V7/II), III7 to VIm (V7/VI), II7 to V (V7/V), I7 to IV (V7/IV). They can have their own ii in front: a **secondary ii–V**.
- **Chain of dominants**: consecutive secondary dominants, each resolving into the next (III7 – VI7 – II7 – V7 – I), as in the bridge of *Rhythm changes*.
- **Modal interchange**: chords borrowed from the minor mode in a major key. The most used are **minor subdominant** chords: IVm, bVImaj7, IIø, and **bVII7** (the "backdoor", which resolves to I).
- **Passing diminished chords**: #IV°7, #I°7, bIII°7… link two chords by half step. They are rootless dominants of the chord they lead to.

## The minor mode

In minor, the tonic is less stable and there are more subdominants. Two details:

- The **V7** in minor comes from harmonic minor (with the major 7, the **leading tone**). In natural minor it would be Vm7, which has hardly any tension.
- **IIø7–V7alt–Im** is the minor ii–V–i: the IIø7 and the V7 with b9 and b13 come from the same scale.

## How to practise it

1. **Hear the functions.** Play I–IV–V–I and I–VIm–IIm–V in several keys and sing the tonic over each chord: on T it sounds stable, on SD it moves away, on D it pulls up or down.
2. **Analyse the repertoire.** Take a standard from the Repertoire and write each chord's degree and function above it. Look for the ii–Vs (and which chord they lead to), the secondary dominants and the borrowed chords. The analyser above does the same with the progressions.
3. **Substitute.** In a progression, replace a chord with another of the same function: I with VIm or IIIm, IV with IIm, V7 with subV7 or VII°7. Listen to what changes and what stays. More in [Reharmonization](../rearmonizacion/).
4. **Add ii–Vs.** Before any chord, put its ii–V. A I–IV becomes I – (Vm7 – I7) – IV. It is the quickest way to "jazz up" a simple progression.
5. **Improvise with the functions.** Over the dominant, aim for the notes that resolve (the 3 and 7 of V7 towards the 1 and 3 of I). Over the tonic, rest on the 3, the 5 or the 9.
6. **Play in every key.** Functions don't depend on the key: the ii–V–I is the same shape in all twelve. Practise it in all twelve with [shell voicings](../shells/) or in [drop 2](../drop2/).

## Ideas for variety

- **Reharmonise a melody.** Take a simple melody and try chords with different functions under the same note.
- **Long chains.** Chain secondary dominants from far away: VII7 – III7 – VI7 – II7 – V7 – I.
- **Major and minor at once.** Play I–IV–V–I and replace IV with IVm: modal interchange in a single chord.
