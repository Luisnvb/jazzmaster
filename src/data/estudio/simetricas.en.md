## Symmetric scales

Almost every scale has a single root: if you move the shape, the notes change. Two don't: the **diminished** and the **whole-tone** scale. They are made of a repeating interval pattern, so when you transpose them a fixed distance you get **the same notes**:

| Scale | Pattern | Repeats every | Different scales |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diminished | half-whole (or whole-half) | 3 frets (minor 3rd) | 3 |
| Whole tone | whole-whole-whole… | 2 frets (a whole step) | 2 |

On the guitar this is a huge advantage: **a shape in one position also works three frets higher** (two for whole tone). On the map above, the dashed box marks where the shape repeats; the ◀ ▶ buttons move the position by that distance.

## The diminished scale

Eight notes alternating half and whole steps. Depending on where you start:

- **Half-whole** (1 b9 #9 3 #11 5 13 b7): over a **dominant**. It has the 3, the 5 and the b7 of the chord and every tension you can add except the b13: b9, #9, #11 and 13. It is the sound of **13b9**.
- **Whole-half** (1 2 b3 4 b5 #5 6 7): over a **°7**. The chord tones and, between them, those of the °7 a whole step above.

Because it repeats every minor 3rd, C half-whole is the same as Eb, Gb and A half-whole. **One shape works for C7, Eb7, Gb7 and A7**. And since °7 is also symmetric, C°7, Eb°7, Gb°7 and A°7 are the same chord.

Things that come straight out of the scale:

- **Four major triads** a minor 3rd apart (in C half-whole: C, Eb, Gb, A) and four minor ones. Arpeggiating two of them is a [triad pair](../pares-triadas/) with a diminished sound.
- **Two °7 chords** that share out the eight notes (C°7 and Db°7 in C's).
- A **dominant with b9** is a °7 with the root underneath: C7b9 = C + E G Bb Db (E°7). That is why the °7 on the dominant's 3 is that dominant's scale.

## Whole tone

Six notes, all a whole step apart: 1 9 3 #11 #5 b7. Over an **augmented dominant** (7#5, 9#5, 7#11#5). It has no 4 and no perfect 5, so **it has no centre**: it sounds floating, dreamlike (Debussy, Monk).

There are only **two** whole-tone scales: C's (C D E F# G# Bb) and Db's. C's works for C7#5, D7#5, E7#5, F#7#5, G#7#5 and Bb7#5.

It contains two augmented triads (C+ and D+), which are its natural [triad pair](../pares-triadas/), and each augmented triad is symmetric too (it repeats every 4 frets, a major 3rd). So an augmented arpeggio and its scale move around the neck by 4 or 2 frets without changing shape.

## Where to use them

| Chord | Scale | When |
|---|---|---|
| 7b9, 13b9 | Diminished H–W | V7 resolving to major, with natural 13; secondary dominants (VI7b9 to IIm7) |
| Passing °7 | Diminished W–H | I–#I°7–IIm7, IIm7–#II°7–IIIm7 |
| 7#5, 9#5 | Whole tone | V7 to I, especially if the #5 rises to the 3 of the next chord |
| 7alt | — | Better the [altered scale](../modos/): it has b9, #9, #11 **and** b13. The diminished has a natural 13 and whole tone a natural 9 |

The rule: listen to which tension the chord carries. **b9 with natural 13**, diminished. **#5 with natural 9**, whole tone. **b9 with b13**, altered.

## How to practise them

1. **A position and its echo.** Play the scale in the marked position and repeat the same shape in the dashed box, saying the degrees: the names change, not the fingers.
2. **By minor thirds.** On a two-bar V7, play the diminished scale in one position and jump 3 frets every half bar: the line rises without leaving the scale.
3. **Triads inside the scale.** In the H–W diminished, arpeggiate C, Eb, Gb and A going up; then C, A, Gb, Eb going down.
4. **Four-note patterns.** Groups of 4 notes that move up a symmetric distance (3 frets for diminished, 2 for whole tone): the pattern and the scale fit together, and the line sounds "composed" even though it is an exercise.
5. **In a cadence.** In IIm7–V7b9–Imaj7, the diminished scale only on the V7, resolving to a chord tone of the next chord: the b9 falls to the 5 of the next chord and the 13 stays as its 3 or falls to the 9.
6. **With a pedal.** Turn on *Pedal note* to hear the scale over its root. Also try C half-whole over an Eb root: the same scale, another chord.
