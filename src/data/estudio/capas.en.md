## The idea

One chord, one area of the neck and everything that fits in it, **layer by layer on the same diagram**: first the roots, then the triads, the arpeggio, the open triads, the pentatonic, the scale and, at the end, the chord that ties it all together. Each layer rests on the previous one, so you never learn a shape in isolation: you learn where it falls in relation to what you already know.

It is an adaptation of [Noel Johnston](https://www.noeljohnston.com/guitar-burpees.html)'s *Guitar Burpees*: a short workout, always the same, repeated every day with another root and another quality.

## The CAGED areas

The five open major chords (**C**, **A**, **G**, **E** and **D**) can be moved up the neck as barre shapes. For any root, each shape falls in a different place, and the five together cover the whole neck, chained in that order (C-A-G-E-D and back to C an octave higher).

Here each shape marks a **six-fret area**: the hand in position (one finger per fret) plus one stretch fret on each side. Everything drawn in the layers is inside that area. For the other qualities you start from the major shape and change its notes: the 3 goes down to b3 in minor chords, the 5 to b5 in diminished ones, and the 7 or the 6 replaces a doubled root (or the 5).

## The layers

1. **Root.** The roots in the area: the anchor points. Everything else is seen in relation to them.
2. **Close triads.** The three chord tones on three adjacent strings (6-5-4, 5-4-3, 4-3-2, 3-2-1), joined by a line. In one area there is usually one per string group, each in a different inversion.
3. **Arpeggio.** The whole chord (with the 7 if it has one) across the area, each pitch only once.
4. **Open triads.** The middle note of the close triad goes up an octave: groups with one string skipped, just above the bass (6-4-3, 5-3-2, 4-2-1) or between the top two (6-5-3, 5-4-2, 4-3-1). They sound wider and are the ones for comping.
5. **Pentatonic.** The one that fits the quality (major on major and dominant chords, minor on minor chords, minor with b5 on m7b5…). Chord tones filled in; the rest hollow.
6. **Scale.** The whole position. Switch between the scales for the quality (Ionian and Lydian on a maj7; Dorian, Aeolian and Phrygian on an m7…) and notice which notes change: the shape is almost the same.
7. **Chord.** The voicing of the CAGED shape. At the end of the round, play it and hear that everything before came from there.

## How to practise

- **One round a day (10–15 min).** Press *Draw*, go through the layers in order and do not move on to the next until you can play the current one without looking, up and down, at a slow tempo.
- **With the layers stacked.** Leave *Stack layers* on: the earlier layers stay dimmed behind, and you can see how each new layer surrounds the previous one. Turn it off to check whether you remember.
- **Name two things.** Say the degree (or, with *Note names*, the name) of each note as you play it.
- **Same root, another shape.** The next day, the same root and quality in the neighbouring shape: the five shapes fit together like pieces and cover the neck.
- **Same shape, another quality.** Change from major to minor or from maj7 to 7 without changing area: you see exactly which notes move (the 3, the 7) and which stay.
- **Listen before you play.** *Play the layer* sounds the whole layer; for the triads and the chord, each voicing arpeggiated and then together. Sing the note before playing it.
